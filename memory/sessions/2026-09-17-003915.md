## What shipped, and what they changed about it

All three proposals landed: `[thread-stale]` as memory-lint check 15 with the `thread_stale_window`
knob (default 40), a review step that reads thread bodies, and the **one lifecycle per record**
anti-pattern named in `DECAY.md` §6.

The maintainer's refinement is better than what we asked for. We proposed an advisory; they made the
rectification a **human closure gate** (REVIEW.md step 8) — the tool surfaces, the owner decides, the
tool never closes a thread — and defined a reset path: only a Memory References entry recording an
owner's decision clears the flag, so parked threads cannot go permanently red. That is the
alert-fatigue lesson from v4.24.0 applied to our own proposal.

Their sharper framing, which we missed: `DECAY.md` §6 *already* said "never-decay ≠ never-checked"
and gave `core` facts and invariants a 40-session human re-check — while unchecked Open Threads, the
third never-decay class **listed in the same section**, had none. The gap was an inconsistency inside
their own documented principle, not merely a missing check.

## Two corrections to our report — both fair

1. **Our headline evidence used the wrong signal.** "7 of 9 untouched for ~117 sessions" came from
   **git dates**, which are a weak proxy: the v4.39.0 migration rewrote every thread file and footer
   refreshes churn them. The reference-based count our own Proposal 1 specified is the right measure,
   and it is what shipped. The conclusion held, but the appendix evidence was softer than it looked —
   a reminder to measure in the system's own units, not a convenient nearby proxy.
2. **The `latest_release` example was target-specific** — the upstream template has no such field. The
   one-lifecycle rule now covers that shape generically, which is the better generalization.

## Two gaps of my own, exposed by the new check

The upgrade ran `memory-lint` and flagged `thread-doc-improvement-feedback-loop` at sslu 111 — a
thread I **merged another thread into** hours earlier. Chasing why produced two findings, both mine:

1. **The seven-thread disposition commit (`006a7f38`) carried no session log at all.** It closed
   three threads, merged two and edited continuity — unambiguously memory-relevant work — and the
   protocol requires a full log for exactly that. This log is the repair.
2. **That thread was never declared under any `## Memory References`.** Not once, in any of the day's
   logs. So the staleness counter correctly read it as untouched, because the counter reads
   references, not file modification.

The second is the more useful lesson: **the new signal is only as good as reference hygiene.** A
counter built on `## Memory References` cannot see work that never declares itself, which is the
failure `conv-declare-consulted-references` was written for after a 42-use fact was demoted the same
way. We asked upstream for a staleness signal and then, the same day, fed it an incomplete input.

Declaring the thread here is a legitimate reset rather than gaming the flag: Eric's "merge the two
doc threads" was an owner decision about that thread, which is precisely what the gate's re-affirm
path records. The two `(blueprint)` gaps flagged at sslu 132 are *not* reset — they go to the gate at
the next review, where closing one is an altitude decision and therefore the owner's.
