## Findings

- **The repository's paths** (38 real ones, and 2 prose fragments both engines refuse), over 1,000 grown and mutated
  documents per path plus 1,000 shared ones: 74,000 evaluations, 19,587 of them non-empty.
  - The engines differ in 170 evaluations on 7 paths, of one kind only: where a path indexes, slices or filters a member
    that is present but not an array (null or text).
  - Jayway answered not found (100) or threw `InvalidPathException` at run time (70); the new engine answers `[]`. That
    is the intended answer: it is the standard's, Mercury's documented shape (only a *missing* name is not found) and
    the Rust engine's.
  - The engines never gave two different non-empty answers.
- **The compliance suite through Jayway** (466 valid queries): Jayway refuses 354, gives the same answer on 91 and a
  different one on 21.
  - Every difference is Jayway departing from the standard: a union of names as an object projection (the guides already
    promised a list), and slices that ignore their step or sign, or misread negative bounds (`$[-5:7]` gave 12 of 10
    elements).
  - Jayway also accepts 133 of the 249 invalid queries.
- **The Jayway-only forms.** All 21 are refused, and their RFC 9535 replacements work. Jayway's own trailing aggregate
  functions threw "Aggregation function attempted to calculate value using empty array" on a three-item list.
- **The benchmark** (38 paths, best of 5×2 s after 3 s of warm-up, three runs, OpenJDK 27 on an Apple M3 Pro):

  | | Jayway | The new engine |
  |---|---|---|
  | evaluate | 412–438 ns | 139–144 ns |
  | compile | 179–183 ns | 196–207 ns |

  Compiling is cached.
