    Test Report — Ask a person driven live through the Playground and both language packs¶

    The first live drive of RFC-0014 (draft-design-specs/ask-a-person-channel.md, build plan step 5): the Java engine's order-assistant and plan-assistant graphs of examples/minigraph-playground with the person.ask hold path, the Python pack's extensions/ask-person (the main flow: the ticket, the resume link, the reply page and endpoint) and extensions/agent-question (the first channel: the store-and-forward inbox on PostgreSQL, the replay, the stream, the automated click), and then the Node.js twins of both, conducted 2026-11-19 (UTC+8) with real Claude calls on Haiku 4.5 through the Python LLM helper. Until this drive the chain had been proven piecewise: the graphs on a scripted model and a mock of person.ask, the two extensions on shared vectors and in-process tests, each pack against a fake of the other side. This is the end-to-end test the RFC's promotion to an ADR was held on.

    The person's device was headless in this drive: no browser automation was available in the session, so the device was a client that holds the stream exactly as the page's script does (Last-Event-ID, the replay first), and the decisions went through the endpoints the page's buttons call (/questions/{id}/decide, /questions/{id}/seen, POST /ask/reply). The page itself was fetched and checked for what it renders. The browser walk-through is the manual regression test at the end.

    Result in brief¶

    • The chain held on every scenario, on both packs. A wait left the engine as one message, reached the inbox before the caller had its advice, appeared on a connected device within 10 ms of the engine staging it, was decided from the device through the pack's reply endpoint, resumed the graph exactly once, and the acknowledgement and the outcome came back to the device as advices. A device that was off found every wait and every advice it had missed in one replay, answered the waits in the opposite order, and the reconnect resumed from its cursor. A late decision met the gate's 410 and the card turned expired; two devices on one ticket both reached the engine and exactly one resumed.
    • Two defects found and fixed. (1) The reply page showed a dead wait as The graph refused with the engine's input check, because the gate recognized only decisions: a re-advice of a dead wait carries the ticket's turn and no decisions, so it ran the fresh path. The gate now treats a request with a turn as a resume attempt too (both graphs, the guide, two new tests). (2) The inbox's decision write was unconditional: with two devices on one card the row took whichever outcome landed last, so a late nothing waiting could overwrite the accepted decision and its operator. A decision's write now applies when the row is still open or when the engine accepted the decision, in both packs (mercury-python #71, mercury-nodejs #136; vectors version 2, a race test on both orderings).
    • Who decided reaches the audit store only for a refusal. The [who at time] stamp travels in the note; the loop sends a refused call's note to the model as the call's error, so it is in the next turn's request, while an approved call's note goes nowhere the model or the audit sees. The decision and its operator are on the inbox row and in the pack's log; carrying the decisions into the turn's audit facts is a loop change listed as a follow-up.
    • A wait decided elsewhere stays open on a device's card until that device tries it (the engine's 410 turns it expired) or the sweeper reaches its expiry; the acknowledgement advice beside it tells the person. Marking it from the advice is a design question for the RFC, not done here.
    • The page's waiting section and history panel (mercury-python #72, mercury-nodejs #137, the same day) hold on both backends. The maintainer's browser pass on the Python backend, and the all-Node chain below on the Node backend: a case stays under Waiting for you while any of its cards is open, moves into the scrollable History panel when the last one closes, the badges and the tab's title count the open cards, and the panel keeps its scroll position across the re-render every event causes.
    • 13 conversations, 35 model turns, 42,951 input and 4,395 output tokens on Haiku 4.5; the inbox ended with 29 rows (9 approvals, 2 questions, 18 advices) and a change sequence of 46; the Python flow served 27 person.ask calls and 17 replies (8 answers, 8 nothing-waiting, one 502 from a negative control with no engine), the Node flow 8 calls and 4 replies. No warning in any pack log, no channel failure, nothing of a prompt or an answer in a log.

    S0 — The pack is down: the hold stands without a link¶

    With ask-person stopped, the question for ORD-1002 (s0-pack-down) returned the approval in 4.70 s with notify_error: Connection refused (connect failed): /127.0.0.1:8091, no link, the waiting issue_refund call and the two answered lookups - the notify-failed handler ran and the run suspended. The pack came back; a re-advice (the same correlation id, {"turn": 2}) answered in 13 ms with the same advice, still without a link and without telling any channel (person.ask was not called: only a fresh approval notifies). The decision straight on the engine resumed the run (1.31 s, the refund issued), and the pack - now up, with no channel configured - logged the acknowledgement and the outcome advices with 0 of 0 channel(s) took it.

    S3 — The device is off, then reconnects¶

    The device was stopped. Three cases ran: an approval (s3-order-a, 3.66 s), a question on plan-assistant (s3-plan-b, 1.98 s, three options drafted by the model), and a third approval (s3-order-c, 3.30 s) decided through the link while the device was off - a refusal with a note, as a person with a chat link would do (1.57 s; the model offered a replacement instead). The inbox then held five rows since the device's cursor: the two open waits, the third wait (still open - the app did not see that decision), and the third case's acknowledgement and outcome advices. The reconnect with Last-Event-ID: 6 replayed exactly those five rows within 14 ms, and GET /questions?since=6 listed the same five with next: 11. The waits were answered in the opposite order: the question first (owner-b, option 2, 3.11 s - the model wrote the plan for the chosen option and its acknowledgement and outcome advices followed), then the approval (supervisor-a, 1.46 s). The third case's card, tried from the device, met the engine's 410 in 34 ms and turned expired with the outcome shown.     S4 — A decision after the suspension expired¶

    The question for ORD-1002 (s4-expiry, 4.02 s) left a card and a Redis record with a ttl of 172,800 s. The record's ttl was set to one second and Redis expired it - what two days would have done. The decision from the card met the gate's 410 in 26 ms and the card turned expired with nothing-waiting as its outcome; the same decision through the link's endpoint got the same 410; the audit store shows the conversation stopped at two turns - no conversation was started by the late decision. The link's GET showed the first defect again (The graph refused, 400); after the fix it shows 410 This wait has expired, the re-advice itself answers 410 in 12 ms on both graphs, and a fresh case with neither a turn nor decisions still meets the input check (400 Missing question).

    S5 — Two devices on one ticket¶

    The question for ORD-1004 (s5-two-devices, 3.52 s), then two decisions on the same card posted at the same moment as device-1 and device-2. Both passed the app's open check and both reached the engine, which restored the record once: device-1 got 200 answer (1.47 s) and device-2 got 410 nothing-waiting (22 ms). A third device after the fact got the app's 409 row 14 is answered - decided by device-1 without an engine call, and the bare link's POST the engine's 410. The stored row read answered by device-1 - but only because the 410 had landed first and the acceptance last: the write was unconditional, and the opposite order would have left the row expired by device-2 over an accepted decision (the second defect). After the fix the race was run twice more (s5b-race-1, s5b-race-2): the losing device's 410 landed first both times (24 ms and 17 ms against 8.23 s and 1.64 s), turned the row expired for a moment, and the acceptance won it back - answered by device-1 with device-1's note, the losing device answered with its own 410. The opposite order is pinned by the race test with a scripted reply endpoint, where the 410 that follows an acceptance changes nothing and the loser sees who decided.

    # Where Defect Fix
    1 the reference graphs (here) the gate recognized only decisions, so the reply page's re-advice of a dead wait (a turn, no decisions) ran the fresh path and the page showed The graph refused with the engine's input check the gate probes turn too (&& of the two probes), both graphs, the guide's snippet and the graph README; aReadviceWithNothingWaitingIsRefused… in both test classes (OrderAssistantTest 9, PlanAssistantTest 5); re-verified live

    Findings and follow-ups¶

    • Who decided is in the audit store only when the note reaches the model. The stamp rides the note; a refusal's note is the call's error and lands in the next turn's request (S3), an approval's note goes nowhere the audit sees. The decision with its operator is on the inbox row (answered_by, decisions) and in the pack's log. The durable fix is the loop carrying the decisions into the resumed turn's facts (RFC-0012's audit facts, an engine and helper change); the RFC's claim is narrowed accordingly.
    • A wait decided elsewhere stays open on another device's card until that device tries it or the sweeper reaches the expiry; the acknowledgement advice for the same turn sits beside it. Closing the card from that advice would need a state the schema does not have (answered elsewhere, no operator known to the app) - a question for the RFC.
    • The health's connected count lags a device that dropped until the next write or keep-alive finds the socket gone (it read 1 for a second after the device stopped). Cosmetic.
    • The demo's order book remembers refunds across cases: a second case on the same lost order gets already refunded from the refund tool and the model says so. A Playground restart resets the book; use ORD-1002 and ORD-1004 in turn.
    • Scenario note: the device's replay after a reconnect and the stream's Last-Event-ID produced identical row sets in every reconnect of the drive; the keep-alive comments never carried a row.

    5. The Playground (terminal 4): java -jar examples/minigraph-playground/target/minigraph-playground-4.12.30.jar after mvn clean package -DskipTests -f examples/minigraph-playground/pom.xml; wait for the health at http://127.0.0.1:8085/health.
    6. Device online. Post the question with a fresh correlation id: curl -s -X POST http://127.0.0.1:8085/api/graph/order-assistant -H 'content-type: application/json' -H 'x-correlation-id: case-1' -d '{"question": "My order ORD-1002 never arrived. Mark them Seen: the whole case moves into the History panel, the badge disappears and the history count grows by one. Scroll the panel, then post the next case: the panel keeps its place while the waiting section updates.
    7. Device off. Close the page. Post two cases: ORD-1004 on order-assistant (case-2) and curl … /api/graph/plan-assistant -H 'x-correlation-id: plan-1' -d '{"task": "Ship the payment-service migration"}'. Reopen the page: both cards are there, the question with its options as radio buttons. Answer the plan first, then the refund; each gets its advices.
    8. The bare link. Post a third case (case-3, ORD-1002), copy link from the reply and open it in a new tab: the pack's own page shows the wait; refuse it with a note and submit - the outcome appears under the form. Back on the app's page the card is still open, with the acknowledgement advice beside it; try to send a decision from it and it turns expired · nothing-waiting. Open the link a second time: This wait has expired.
    9. After expiry. Post case-4, then docker exec mercury-redis redis-cli expire graph:order-assistant:case-4 1 and wait two seconds. Decide the card: expired · nothing-waiting; the link shows This wait has expired.
    10. Two devices. Post case-5 and open the app in a second browser (or a private window) under another name. Decide the same card from both as close together as you can: one browser shows answered · decided by its name and the other the 410 outcome; after a moment both show the row answered by the first. A third attempt is refused by the app: row N is answered - decided by ….
    11. Evidence. pgweb at http://127.0.0.1:8081: select correlation_id, kind, state, answered_by, outcome->>'status' from question_inbox order by id for the cards, and select correlation_id, turn, status, left(request::text, 200) from transcript_turn order by conversation, turn for the turns - the refusal's [name at time] stamp is in the request of the turn that followed it.

    Before promotion¶

    Everything step 5 asked for is driven, the two defects are fixed in the repositories they belong to, and the RFC's build notes record the findings. What remains for the RFC is step 6 (the chat, mail and topic examples, when a server is available to drive) and the promotion to an ADR on the maintainer's call, with the two follow-ups above as the loop's and the app's open items.
